# Kitguns — the design, and the finding that made it cheap

A Kitgun is not a weapon this roster can hold, and `docs/WEAPON_INTAKE.md` says
why: it has no published stat line of its own, only a **chamber**, a **grip**
and a **loader** that compute one. Filing a chamber as a weapon would ship an
arbitrary grip's numbers under a name no player can reproduce.

This is the shape it takes instead.

## THE FINDING: the parts are DATA and the composition is PUBLISHED

The wiki's Kitgun page shows a stat table per chamber, and those tables looked
like the reason to stay away — 6 chambers × 10 grips × 20 loaders is **1,200
combinations**. They are not written by hand. They are generated by
`Module:Modular` from `Module:Modular/data`, which is a structured Lua table of
the same kind as `Module:Weapons/data` that this repo already reads.

So the cost is **42 part records and one composition rule**, not 1,200 rows:

| part | count | what it carries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chamber | 6 × 2 slots = 12 | crit, status, accuracy, ammo, spread, trigger, shot type, falloff, forced procs, range, multishot, disposition — plus a **damage table indexed by GRIP**, a **fire-rate table indexed by GRIP**, and a **magazine table indexed by SIZE CLASS** |
| Grip | 10 (5 primary, 5 secondary) | which SLOT the weapon is, and `Recoil`. Its effect on damage and fire rate is already resolved into the chamber's per-grip tables |
| Loader | 20 | `CritChance` +Δ, `CritMultiplier` +Δ, `StatusChance` +Δ, a magazine **size-class name**, and `Reload` in absolute seconds |

The rule is exact — there is nothing to approximate:

```
damage       = chamber.Damage[grip]              # per grip, published
fire_rate    = chamber.FireRate[grip]            # per grip, published
magazine     = chamber.Magazine[loader.Magazine] # size class -> a number
crit_chance  = chamber.CritChance     + loader.CritChance
crit_mult    = chamber.CritMultiplier + loader.CritMultiplier
status       = chamber.StatusChance   + loader.StatusChance
reload       = loader.Reload
everything else is the chamber's
```

**THE SLOT IS NOT A LABEL ON THE CHAMBER.** `KitgunSecondary` and
`KitgunPrimary` are two blocks with their own chamber data, and the difference
reaches the DAMAGE TYPE: a secondary Gaze deals Puncture + Radiation, a primary
Gaze deals Radiation alone. A chamber must therefore be stored per slot, and
"the Gaze" is two records rather than one with a flag.

## WHAT IT IS IN THIS ARCHITECTURE: a CUSTOM that produces a WEAPON

AGENTS.md already has the category. A **custom** is a thing you MADE that the
OTHER modules consume — a riven becomes a mod in the pool, a custom enemy
becomes an entry in the scenario's target list, and neither needed the three
modules to learn anything about it. A Kitgun is the third, and what it produces
is a **`WeaponSpec`**: the builder lists it, the simulator fights with it, the
optimizer searches it, with no Kitgun-specific code in any of them.

That is the whole test a new editor tab has to pass, and it passes it.

Consequences that follow from being a custom rather than a preset:

- **It is not weapon-scoped**, because it IS a weapon. `wfsim-customs-kitguns`,
  one list for the account, the same as `enemies`.
- **Owning none is ordinary.** Nothing is auto-created and the last one can be
  deleted; the editor stands down rather than showing a document that is not
  there.
- **Deleting one breaks references elsewhere** — a build that equipped it, a
  scenario that never mentions it. That is the difference between a custom and
  a preset, and the riven editor's delete already has the shape to copy.
- **A share link must carry it inline**, exactly as it carries a riven: a custom
  exists only on the machine that made it.

## THREE THINGS THE OWNER DECIDES

**1. Does a Kitgun reach the BOARD?** A board row is "a build you can re-run",
and a Kitgun IS reproducible from three part names — a shorter identity than
most builds carry. If yes, `builds::BUILD_AXES` gains the assembly, and the
worker's `AXES` table and the share tuple gain it with it (the axis is declared
once; the spellings stay per protocol). If no, the editor is for the builder and
the simulator only, and the optimizer cannot rank one either.

**2. Does the optimizer search the ASSEMBLY?** The builder binds one chamber,
one grip, one loader; the optimizer binds a SET, which is the same relation it
has to every other axis (memory: the optimizer binds a set where the builder
binds a value). 600 assemblies per slot is a small scope beside a mod pool, and
"which loader" is exactly the question a player cannot answer by reading.

**3. What is a Kitgun's NAME?** Players name theirs. A URL mirrors an English
wiki page name — but a custom has no wiki page. Either the editor lives at a
fixed `/kitguns` and an assembly is addressed by its parts, or a named Kitgun
gets a slug of its own. The first invents no names.

## THE EXCLUSIVE ARCANES

A gilded Kitgun seats a **Pax** arcane, and Kitguns and Zaws share the
**Residual** family. Eight in total, and they split cleanly by whether this
simulator has the thing they act on:

| arcane | effect | here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pax Charge** | changes the magazine to a rechargeable battery, "like that of the Cycron" | **MODELLABLE, and the mechanic already exists** — the roster has battery weapons and the transmute/refill path they use |
| **Pax Seeker** | on headshot kill, releases homing projectiles for 50% Puncture / 50% Slash | **MODELLABLE** — it is an EXTRA HIT by `docs/EXTRA_HIT.md`'s own definition: a second damage instance beside a hit, worth a percentage of it, rolling its own status |
| **Pax Bolt** | on headshot kill, +Ability Strength and Efficiency for the next ability | **OUT OF SCOPE** — the Warframe layer, and `docs/UNMODELLED.md` already holds that open |
| **Pax Soar** | while airborne: accuracy, recoil, aim glide, wall latch | **MOSTLY OUT** — recoil and aim glide are handling; `airborne` IS a scenario field, so the accuracy half is reachable if the owner wants it |
| **Residual Boils / Malodor / Shock / Viremia** | on headshot kill, a lingering ground effect | **NOT YET READ** — they are the shape of a `lingering:` area, which the engine has, but each needs its own transcription |

Two are worth having on the day the editor lands, two are admissions, and four
need a read.

## RIVENS: one riven, two dispositions

A Kitgun riven is for a **chamber**, and the same riven equips on both the
primary and the secondary build of it — but the DISPOSITION differs by slot
(the Tombfinger is 1.00x as a primary and 0.85x as a secondary), and the wiki
notes that a riven's printed stats default to the primary's.

This roster already has the riven editor and `rivens_data::derived_for`; what it
does not have is a riven whose disposition depends on what the weapon was
assembled AS. That is one field, and it is the only place the two-slot problem
reaches a system other than the weapon itself.

## WHAT THIS COSTS

- **Data**: 42 part records, transcribed from a structured module the repo
  already knows how to read, plus a survey and a ratchet in the shape of
  `scripts/survey_*.py` — the composition is exact, so the ratchet can assert
  that every one of the 1,200 assemblies reproduces the wiki's own table.
- **Engine**: a `kitgun` module that resolves three parts into a `WeaponSpec`,
  and nothing else. The three modules stay ignorant, which is the point.
- **Page**: one editor tab, built from the riven editor's list + editor shape.
- **Arcanes**: two on day one, two admissions, four to read.

The 1,200 assemblies are not the work. They are the reason the work is small:
every one of them is a test case with a published answer.
